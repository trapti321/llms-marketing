01 · The Basics

What SEO services actually include.

Search engine optimization is the work of making a website easy for search engines to crawl, understand and trust, and useful enough that people choose it. Google's own page on whether you need an SEO lists the services a good SEO company provides: reviews of site content and structure, technical advice on hosting, redirects, error pages and JavaScript, content development, keyword research, expertise in specific markets, and now optimizing for generative AI.

That last item matters more each year, but it is not a separate discipline. Google says there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode: a page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet, and the usual SEO fundamentals apply. A slow, poorly linked or thin site is invisible to both.

So our SEO program is built for both surfaces at once. If you mainly need content rewritten and produced for AI citation, our AI SEO and content service is the focused version of that layer.

03 · Prioritization

How we decide what to do first.

Most sites have more SEO issues than any budget can fix in a quarter. Audit tools will happily flag hundreds of warnings, and Google itself notes that third-party tools have no access to its internal ranking data. We rank work by likely business impact, then effort, in this order.

1. Anything blocking indexing

Pages that cannot be crawled or indexed cannot rank or appear in AI features. Robots rules, noindex tags, broken canonicals and redirect chains on money pages get fixed before anything else.

2. Pages closest to revenue

Service, product and location pages that already rank on page two for buying searches usually move faster than new content. Improving them is often the cheapest win available.

3. Gaps your buyers search for

Next come the questions and comparisons buyers research before they choose, written so both a person and an AI system can lift a clear answer. Our step-by-step guide to SEO for a website shows the same order for teams doing it in house.

4. Authority to compete

When the pages are right but competitors still outrank you, the gap is usually links and mentions. That is when authority work earns its budget.

05 · Timelines

What timelines are realistic.

Google's SEO starter guide is direct about timing: some changes take effect in a few hours, others can take several months, and you should generally wait a few weeks before judging whether a change helped. It also notes that not every change produces a visible effect. Any SEO company promising a fixed ranking by a fixed date is guessing.

06 · Choosing an SEO Company

How to vet an SEO agency, using Google's checklist.

Hiring the wrong SEO company can do real damage. Google warns that if an SEO creates deceptive or misleading content on your behalf, your site could be removed from its index, and that you are responsible for the actions of anyone you hire. Its guidance gives a practical way to screen agencies, including us.

Questions to ask

  • Can you show examples of previous work and put me in touch with past clients?
  • Do you follow Google Search Essentials, and do you cite Google's documentation for your recommendations?
  • What results do you expect, in what timeframe, and how do you measure success?
  • Will you share every change you make to my site and explain why?
  • If you offer AI search (often sold as "AEO" or "GEO"), does your advice match Google's guidance on generative AI features?

Red flags Google names

  • Guaranteed first-place rankings. Google says no one can guarantee a number one ranking.
  • Claims of a "special relationship" with Google or a "priority submit."
  • Secrecy about methods, or requests that you link to the SEO's site.
  • Unsolicited emails, link popularity schemes and submissions to "thousands of search engines."
  • Asking for write access during an audit. Google recommends granting read access to Search Console only at that stage.

If you feel an SEO deceived you, Google points US businesses to the Federal Trade Commission. For a sector-specific version of this checklist, see how to choose a law firm SEO company.

10 · FAQ

SEO services: common questions.

How much do SEO services cost?

It depends on the size of your site, how competitive your market is and how much content and link work is needed. In Ahrefs' survey of SEO professionals, monthly SEO usually ranged from $250 to $10,000, most businesses spent $500 to $5,000, and agencies charged $3,209 a month on average. We scope every engagement after a free review, so you see the plan, the deliverables and the price before you commit.

How long does SEO take to work?

Google says some changes take effect in a few hours while others can take several months, and suggests waiting a few weeks before judging whether a change helped. In practice, technical fixes and improvements to pages that already rank tend to show first, while new content and authority building take longer. We set expectations for each workstream in writing and report progress monthly.

Can you guarantee first-page rankings?

No, and you should be wary of anyone who does. Google's own guidance says no one can guarantee a number one ranking and warns against SEOs who claim a special relationship with Google. What we commit to is the work: a clear plan, documented changes, methods that follow Google Search Essentials and honest reporting on rankings, traffic and leads.

What is the difference between your SEO services and AI SEO?

Our SEO service is the full program: technical SEO, on-page SEO, content, link building, local SEO and measurement. Our AI SEO and content service focuses on producing and restructuring content so AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode can understand and cite it. Many clients run both, with AI SEO as the content layer on top of the SEO program.

Do I need separate optimization for Google AI Overviews and AI Mode?

Google says there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and no special files or schema are needed. A page has to be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet, so the same technical, content and trust work that drives organic rankings is what makes a page eligible for AI features. That is why we treat them as one program.

Do you build backlinks?

We earn links rather than buy them. Google's spam policies treat buying or selling links for ranking purposes as link spam. Our authority work focuses on content worth citing, digital PR, partner and supplier relationships, local and industry associations, and fixing links you have already earned that point to broken pages.

Will I own the work and the data?

Yes. Your Google Search Console, Google Analytics and Business Profile stay in your business's name, with us added as users. Content we write and pages we build for you are yours, and every change is recorded in a log you can keep if you ever change providers.
