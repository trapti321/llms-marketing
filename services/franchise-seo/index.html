01 · The Franchise Split

Why franchise SEO is harder than single-location SEO.

A single business owns its website, its Business Profile and its reviews. A franchise system splits those assets between two parties with different goals. The franchisor wants brand consistency and national demand. Each franchisee wants calls and bookings in their own territory, often this month. When nobody writes down who controls what, the same problems show up in almost every system we look at.

Brand site vs location pages

The franchisor usually owns the domain, but franchisees know the local details that make a location page worth reading. If corporate writes every page from a template, pages go thin. If every franchisee builds their own microsite, the brand ends up with dozens of competing domains and no shared authority.

Who owns the Business Profiles

Profiles get claimed by whoever got there first: a franchisee, a former manager, a past agency. That makes bulk changes, holiday hours and brand updates slow or impossible, and it puts verification at risk when people leave.

Co-op and local budgets

Many systems fund marketing through a brand or co-op fund plus local spend. Franchise SEO services work best when the fund pays for shared infrastructure (site architecture, templates, schema, reporting) and franchisees invest in what only they can supply: local photos, community proof and review requests.

03 · Location Pages

Scaling location pages without doorway content.

Location pages are the core of multi-location SEO, and they are also where franchise sites get into trouble. Google's spam policies define doorway abuse as pages "created to rank for specific, similar search queries" that lead users to intermediate pages less useful than the final destination. The listed examples include pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page, and "substantially similar pages that are closer to search results than a clearly defined, browseable hierarchy" (Google Search Central).

The same policy page also covers scaled content abuse: many pages generated to manipulate rankings rather than help people, including pages generated with AI tools "without adding value for users." A franchise site with 300 pages that differ only by city name fits both descriptions.

What makes a location page worth indexing

Each page should exist because a real location exists, and should answer what a customer of that location needs: the exact address and service area, hours, the services that location actually offers, the local team, parking or access notes, location-specific reviews and photos, and a direct path to call or book. We build one template with fields that force those differences, then fill them with input from each franchisee.

Hierarchy and structured data

Pages sit in a browseable path from brand to state or metro hub to location, not a flat list of city URLs. Each page carries LocalBusiness structured data, which Google says can tell it about business hours, departments within a business and more (Google), and links to its own Business Profile. Cities with no physical location are handled honestly, with service-area content on the nearest location page rather than an invented office. 04 · Google Business Profile

Business Profiles for 10+ locations.

For a franchise, the Business Profile is often the first thing a customer sees, before the website. Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence, and that prominence draws on information such as how many websites link to a business and how many reviews it has (Google Business Profile Help). Distance you cannot change. Relevance and prominence you can, location by location.

Bulk verification

Google offers bulk verification through Business Profile Manager for businesses with 10 or more locations of the same business. Google's requirements are specific: the spreadsheet must include every profile you manage, the business cannot be a service-area business, agencies with more than one business in one account cannot bulk verify, and duplicate, suspended or disabled profiles do not count toward the minimum. Google also notes it may de-verify accounts that do not actively manage their profiles (Google Business Profile Help). Service-area franchises, such as home services brands, verify location by location instead, which we plan for up front.

Business groups and naming

Google recommends business groups as a safer way to share access than sharing passwords, and notes that a manager added to a group can access all current and future profiles in it (Google). Its guidelines also require chains to use the same name and category across locations unless the real-world branding consistently differs, and allow an authorized "franchisee" to use the underlying brand name (Google guidelines). We audit every profile against those rules, fix duplicates and set up a structure where corporate stays in control.

05 · Reviews at Scale

Review management across every location.

Reviews are where franchise systems most often break the rules without meaning to. Google's policy prohibits offering incentives for reviews and also prohibits businesses from discouraging negative reviews or "selectively" soliciting positive ones (Google Maps content policy). That rules out review gating, where only happy customers are sent the review link. It also rules out employee reviews, which Google treats as a conflict of interest.

US law adds teeth. The FTC's final rule on consumer reviews, announced in August 2024, bans fake reviews, undisclosed insider reviews, review suppression through threats or intimidation, and incentives "conditioned on the writing of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment," and lets the FTC seek civil penalties against knowing violators (FTC). One franchisee running a gift card promotion can put the brand at risk.

Our approach is a system-wide review program: every real customer gets the same request after a completed job or visit, sent automatically through your CRM or POS with marketing automation. Replies follow a shared library with brand voice and escalation rules, and franchisees personalize them. Google notes that replying to reviews shows customers you value their feedback (Google). For tactics a single location can use, see our guide on how to get more Google reviews.

06 · AI Answers

Showing up in AI answers for "near me" brand searches.

Customers now ask Google's AI Mode, ChatGPT and Gemini questions like "is there a [brand] near me open Sunday" or "best [service] franchise in Columbus." For Google's AI features, Google says there are no additional requirements beyond normal SEO: a page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet. Its listed best practices include internal links, textual content, structured data that matches the visible page, and "checking that your Merchant Center and Business Profile information is up-to-date" (Google Search Central).

For a franchise, that means the basics done consistently across hundreds of locations: crawlable location pages with hours, services and addresses in plain text, schema that matches, and Business Profiles that agree with the website. Inconsistent hours or a closed location still listed as open are the kind of errors that surface in AI answers. 11 · FAQ

Franchise SEO: common questions.

What is franchise SEO?

Franchise SEO is search engine optimization for a brand and all of its locations at once. It covers the brand website, a location page for each real location, a verified Google Business Profile for each location, reviews, local links and reporting by location, with clear rules on what the franchisor and each franchisee are responsible for.

How is franchise SEO different from local SEO?

Local SEO helps one business rank in its area. Franchise SEO repeats that work across many locations while keeping the brand consistent, so it adds governance between corporate and franchisees, shared templates, bulk Business Profile management, system-wide review policies and reporting that rolls up from location to region to brand.

Should franchisees have their own websites?

Usually not. Separate franchisee microsites split authority, duplicate content and make brand changes hard. Most systems do better with location pages on the brand domain, each with unique local content supplied by the franchisee. Google's spam policies also warn against multiple sites or near-identical city pages built only to rank.

Who should own the Google Business Profiles in a franchise?

We recommend the franchisor's business group as primary owner, with each franchisee added as a manager of their own location. That keeps profiles with the brand if a franchisee, employee or agency leaves, while franchisees can still update hours, photos and review replies.

Can we verify all of our locations at once?

Often, yes. Google offers bulk verification in Business Profile Manager for businesses with 10 or more locations of the same business. The spreadsheet must include every location you manage, and service-area businesses are not eligible, so home services franchises usually verify each location individually.

Can franchisees offer discounts for Google reviews?

No. Google's policy prohibits incentives for reviews and selectively asking only happy customers. The FTC's 2024 rule on consumer reviews also bans incentives conditioned on a particular sentiment, fake reviews and undisclosed insider reviews. A compliant program asks every real customer the same way, with no reward attached.

How long does franchise SEO take to show results?

Fixes such as resolving duplicate profiles, correcting hours and repairing broken location pages can show up within weeks. Rankings and leads from new location pages and review growth usually take several months and vary by market competition. We pilot in a few locations first so you can see results before a full rollout, and we never guarantee rankings.
