01: The Honest Version

What online reputation management really is.

Online reputation management (ORM) is the work of shaping what people find when they search for your business or your name: search results, review sites, social profiles, news coverage and, increasingly, AI answers. A good reputation management agency does three things. It removes what can legitimately be removed, it responds to what cannot, and it builds enough accurate, positive material that one bad result stops defining you.

The industry has a reputation problem of its own. Some ORM companies sell "guaranteed deletion," flood the web with fake reviews or threaten reviewers. Those tactics break platform rules and, since the Federal Trade Commission's rule on fake reviews and testimonials, can break federal law too. The FTC announced the final rule in August 2024 and said it lets the agency seek civil penalties against knowing violators (FTC).

So we start every engagement by telling you plainly what is possible for your situation, and what is not.

02: Removal Routes

How to remove negative search results the legitimate way.

Google does remove some content from its results, but only through specific routes with specific rules. Knowing which one applies saves months of wasted effort.

Personal contact info: Results about you

Google's Results about you tool finds search results that show your home address, phone number or email and lets you request their removal, with alerts when new ones appear. Google says it will not remove information that is valuable to the public, such as pages from government or educational sites and newspapers. Its wider personal information policy also covers government ID numbers, bank details, confidential records and doxxing, and it notes that removal from Google does not remove the page from the web.

Deleted or changed pages: Refresh Outdated Content

If a site has already taken a page down or removed the damaging part, but Google still shows the old snippet, the Refresh Outdated Content tool asks Google to update its result. Google is clear that the tool will fail if the information is still on the live page.

Unlawful content: legal removal requests

Defamation, copyright infringement and court orders go through Google's legal removals process, which asks for the exact URLs and a specific explanation. We prepare the evidence and work alongside your attorney. We are not a law firm and do not give legal advice.

Everything else: the website owner

Most negative results are lawful, accurate or opinion. Google will not remove them, so the realistic options are asking the publisher for a correction or update, responding publicly, and outranking the result with better pages.

03: Review Management

Review management that follows the platforms' rules.

Reviews are where most reputations are won or lost, and every platform has its own rules about which reviews can be removed and how businesses may ask for them.

Flagging reviews that break policy

Google removes reviews that violate its Maps content policy, including fake engagement, reviews posted by competitors and reviews with a conflict of interest. Google's guide to reporting inappropriate reviews also says not to report a review just because you disagree with it, that evaluation typically takes several days, and that you get one appeal if a report is rejected. We document violations properly, so the reports we file have a real chance.

Review extortion

If a sudden wave of 1-star reviews arrives with a demand for money, Google has a dedicated extortion report and tells businesses not to pay or engage. We help you gather the evidence it asks for.

Asking for reviews, platform by platform

Google lets businesses ask customers for reviews but prohibits incentives and selectively asking only happy customers. Yelp's content guidelines go further: businesses should never ask customers to write reviews at all. That difference matters, and our comparison of Google reviews vs Yelp explains it. For Google, our guide on how to get more Google reviews covers review links, timing and replies.

What the FTC rule bans

The FTC's rule, codified at 16 CFR Part 465, prohibits fake or AI-generated reviews from people who do not exist or never used the business, incentives conditioned on positive or negative sentiment, undisclosed reviews by officers and managers, company-controlled "independent" review sites, and review suppression through groundless legal threats, intimidation or hiding negative reviews while presenting the rest as complete.

04: Our Rules

What we will never do for your reputation.

A short-term fix that gets your Business Profile restricted or draws an FTC complaint is not reputation management. These are the lines we do not cross, whatever the budget:

  • We do not buy reviews, write reviews or use AI to generate reviews for you.
  • We do not offer customers rewards for positive reviews or "gate" requests so only happy customers are asked.
  • We do not create fake articles, fake profiles or sock-puppet accounts to bury results.
  • We do not threaten reviewers or report reviews simply because they are negative.
  • We do not promise that any result or review will be deleted. Only Google, the platform or a court can decide that.

What we do instead is slower but durable: legitimate requests where they apply, honest replies, more genuine reviews and better content that you own.

05: Your Brand SERP

Own the first page for your name.

Your brand SERP is the page Google shows when someone searches your business or your name. When a negative result cannot be removed, the honest answer is to give Google better, more relevant results to show above it. That is ordinary SEO applied to your name, and it works best with real assets:

Pages you control

A clear About page, leadership and team bios, a press or news page, and service pages that answer the questions people search alongside your name, such as pricing, complaints or reviews.

Profiles that rank for names

A verified Google Business Profile, LinkedIn company and founder profiles, and active profiles on the review and industry directories that matter in your category. For local businesses this overlaps heavily with local SEO.

Clear entity signals

Organization and Person schema, consistent names, and links between your official profiles help search engines and AI tools understand which results are really about you. Our guide to schema and entity optimization explains how.

Earned coverage

Genuine interviews, guest articles and industry mentions carry more weight than anything you publish yourself, and they give the story a fair second side.

06: AI Answers

What AI answers say about your brand.

People now ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode questions like "is this company legit?" or "what do reviews say about this dentist?" before they ever visit your site. Those answers are built from sources on the web, so an old complaint thread or an outdated directory listing can shape them for months.

There is no form to edit an AI answer directly. Google says there are no additional requirements or special optimizations needed to appear in AI Overviews and AI Mode, and that supporting links come from pages that are indexed and eligible to show in Search. In practice, that means AI reputation work is source work:

  • We run a fixed set of brand prompts across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI features each month and record what they say and which sources they cite.
  • We trace wrong facts back to the pages feeding them, such as old directory listings, stale press releases or your own outdated pages, and correct them at the source.
  • We publish clear, factual pages that answer the questions AI tools are asked about you, and keep official profiles current, including your ChatGPT Business Hub listing.

This is the same discipline as our AI search visibility service, pointed at reputation. Our guide on getting your brand recommended by ChatGPT covers the wider picture.

10: FAQ

Online reputation management: common questions.

What do online reputation management services include?

Online reputation management services cover the search results, reviews, profiles and AI answers people see about your business or name. Typical work includes a reputation audit, legitimate removal requests, flagging reviews that break platform policies, review responses, compliant review generation, owned content for your brand search results, profile and schema cleanup, and monitoring of reviews, mentions and AI answers.

Can you remove negative search results from Google?

Sometimes. Google removes results that show personal contact information or sensitive data through Results about you and its personal information policy, updates results for pages already deleted or changed through the Refresh Outdated Content tool, and reviews legal requests for things like defamation or copyright. Lawful, accurate or opinion content usually stays, so the realistic option is to respond and outrank it with better pages.

Can you delete negative reviews?

Only the platform can remove a review, and only when it breaks that platform's rules, such as fake engagement, conflicts of interest or content from competitors. We document and report reviews that genuinely violate policy and use the appeal where one is allowed. Reviews that are simply negative stay up, so we help you reply well and earn more genuine reviews.

Do you buy reviews or post fake content?

No. We do not buy reviews, write or AI-generate reviews, offer rewards for positive reviews, gate review requests, create fake profiles or articles, or threaten reviewers. These tactics break Google and Yelp policies, and the FTC's rule on fake reviews and testimonials prohibits fake reviews, sentiment-based incentives, undisclosed insider reviews and review suppression.

How long does online reputation management take?

Platform requests and review reports usually get a decision within days to weeks. New owned pages and profiles typically take a few months to rank for your name, depending on how competitive the results are. Review counts grow at the pace you serve customers and ask them, so most reputation programs are judged over three to six months.

Can you change what ChatGPT or Google AI Overviews say about my business?

Not directly, because there is no form to edit an AI answer. AI tools draw on sources across the web, so we track your brand prompts monthly, trace inaccurate claims to the pages behind them, correct those sources where possible and publish clear, factual pages and profiles. Google says no special optimization is needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode beyond being indexed and eligible to show in Search.

How much do reputation management services cost?

It depends on the problem. A single outdated result may need one request, while years of thin reviews plus a complaint ranking for your name needs months of ongoing work. We scope every engagement after a free audit, so you see the plan and price before committing. Be cautious of any agency that charges per guaranteed removal, because only Google, the platform or a court can decide what comes down.
