01 · Why Category Pages

Why category pages are your real revenue pages.

Ecommerce SEO is the work of making an online store's categories, products and supporting content easy for search engines to crawl, understand and show to shoppers, then turning that visibility into orders. Most stores pour effort into individual product pages. The bigger wins usually sit one level up.

A shopper searching "waterproof women's hiking boots" or "leather sectional sofa" wants to compare options, not land on a single SKU. That is the job of a category page. Category pages also hold your site together: Google says it analyzes the links between pages to understand their relative importance, recommends linking from menus to categories, categories to subcategories and subcategories to every product, and notes that Googlebot generally does not type into your search box. If a product is only reachable through site search, Google may never find it.

So we start with the category tree: which categories match how people search, which deserve their own page, and how each one links down to products and across to related ranges.

04 · Faceted Navigation

Faceted navigation and crawl budget.

Filters for size, color, price and brand help shoppers, but they can quietly wreck a store's SEO. Google's own guide to managing faceted navigation URLs says parameter-based filters can generate infinite URL spaces, leading crawlers to fetch huge numbers of filter URLs before they learn those URLs are useless, which leaves less time for new, useful pages.

Google's recommendations are specific. If you do not need filtered URLs indexed, block them from crawling with robots.txt, or use URL fragments, which Google generally ignores. If you do want some filtered pages indexed, keep filter order consistent, prevent duplicate filters and return a real 404 status when a combination has no results, rather than redirecting to a generic error page.

Crawl budget matters most for bigger catalogs. Google's crawl budget guide is aimed at sites with more than a million unique pages that change weekly, or more than 10,000 pages that change daily, and it calls duplicate and unwanted URLs the factor you can control most. Smaller stores still benefit, because the same duplicates split links and confuse which URL should rank.

Our rule of thumb

A filter earns an indexable page only when people actually search for it ("waterproof hiking boots") and it shows enough products to be useful. That page then gets a static URL, its own title and intro, and links from the parent category. Sorts, view counts and stacked combinations stay out of the index.

05 · Product Data

Product schema and Merchant Center free listings.

Google offers two ways to give it rich product data: structured data on your pages, and a product feed in Merchant Center. Its product structured data overview says providing both "maximizes your eligibility" for shopping experiences and helps Google understand and verify your data.

Merchant listing markup on product pages

For pages where shoppers can buy, Google's merchant listing documentation covers Product markup with an Offer for price and availability, plus shipping details and a return policy. Pages with this markup can be eligible for the shopping knowledge panel, Google Images, popular product results and product snippets. We generate it from your product data so price and stock always match the page, with ProductGroup markup for variants.

Free listings in Merchant Center

According to Google Merchant Center Help, free listings let your products appear at no cost on Google Search, Google Maps, Gemini, YouTube, the Shopping tab, Google Images and Google Lens. New Merchant Center accounts are opted in by default. Free listings point to product pages, not category pages, and eligibility does not guarantee your products are shown, so titles, GTINs, brand, size and images do real work.

One URL per variant

Google's ecommerce URL structure guide warns that it does not use fragment identifiers in indexing, so /t-shirt#black and /t-shirt#white are treated as the same page. If a color or size deserves to be found, it needs a URL that can be crawled on its own.

06 · Stock Changes

Out-of-stock and discontinued products.

Deleting a page the day a product sells out throws away its links and history; leaving thousands of dead products live creates thin pages. We use a simple decision path.

SituationWhat we do
Temporarily out of stockKeep the page live, mark availability as OutOfStock or BackOrder, show a restock date or email signup and link to alternatives
Pre-order or coming soonPublish the page early with PreOrder availability so it can be crawled before launch
Discontinued, with a close replacement301 redirect to the replacement or the closest category
Discontinued, still searched and linkedKeep it as an information page marked Discontinued, pointing to the successor model
Discontinued, no demand, no replacementReturn a 404 or 410 so it drops out of the index

This follows Google's documentation. The merchant listing guide lists availability values including OutOfStock, BackOrder, PreOrder and Discontinued. Google's redirects guide covers permanent 301 redirects, and its HTTP status code documentation says URLs returning a 4xx status are removed from the index. A 200 page showing an error or empty content is flagged as a soft 404.

07 · Reviews

Product reviews that actually help shoppers.

For buying guides and comparisons, Google's guidance on writing high quality reviews asks for evaluation from a user's perspective, evidence of first-hand experience such as your own photos, measurements, comparisons with alternatives, honest benefits and drawbacks, and a reason why anything called "best" is best. We build guides around your team's real product knowledge, then link them to the relevant categories.

For customer reviews, we set up post-purchase review requests and mark up genuine ratings on product pages. We never write, buy or filter reviews: the FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials, effective October 21, 2024, prohibits fake reviews, buying positive or negative reviews and certain review suppression practices.

Reviews also feed conversion. If your product pages get traffic but few sales, our CRO audit guide shows how to find where shoppers drop off.

08 · AI Shopping

Visibility in AI shopping answers.

Google AI Mode

Google says its AI Mode shopping experience combines Gemini with the Shopping Graph, which had more than 50 billion product listings with details like reviews, prices, color options and availability when it was announced in May 2025. Google also announced agentic checkout that can buy a tracked product at a shopper's target price. The Shopping Graph runs on product data, so complete feeds and accurate markup are the foundation.

ChatGPT shopping

OpenAI's merchant page says shopping in ChatGPT is live for users in the US, that merchants can apply to share product feeds, and that Shopify and Etsy catalogs are already integrated with no extra setup. OpenAI says a feed is not required because ChatGPT also crawls sites, but feeds give more control over accuracy. OpenAI also says purchases are completed on the merchant's own website or app, with no fees on purchases that start in ChatGPT.

We cannot guarantee any store appears in an AI answer, and nobody honestly can. What we can do is make your product data complete, consistent and crawlable everywhere it is read. 11 · FAQ

Ecommerce SEO services: common questions.

What do ecommerce SEO services include?

Ecommerce SEO services cover the technical health of your store, keyword research mapped to your category structure, category and product page optimization, faceted navigation rules, product structured data, Merchant Center free listings, buying guide content and reporting on organic orders and revenue.

How much do ecommerce SEO services cost?

It depends on catalog size, platform, how much developer support you have and how many categories need new content. A small Shopify store and a large custom catalog need very different amounts of work. We scope every engagement after a free audit, so you see the plan and the price before you commit.

How long does ecommerce SEO take to show results?

Technical fixes such as indexing and redirect errors can show up within weeks once Google recrawls the pages. Category content, new pages and links usually take several months to build momentum, and competitive categories take longer. We report monthly on organic orders and revenue.

Should category pages or product pages be the SEO priority?

For most stores, category pages. They match broader commercial searches such as a type of product, they link to many products at once and they hold your site structure together. Product pages still matter for model searches and free listings, so we optimize both.

How should faceted navigation be handled for SEO?

Google recommends blocking crawling of filter URLs you do not need indexed, using robots.txt or URL fragments, and following best practices for any you do want indexed, such as consistent filter order and a 404 status for empty results. We give indexable pages only to filters people actually search for and block sorts and stacked combinations.

What should I do with out-of-stock or discontinued products?

Keep temporarily out-of-stock pages live with accurate availability and alternatives. Redirect discontinued products with a close replacement to that product or the nearest category. Return a 404 or 410 for discontinued products with no demand and no replacement, rather than redirecting everything to the homepage, which Google can treat as a soft 404.

Can you get my products into ChatGPT and Google AI Mode shopping results?

No one can guarantee placement in AI answers. Google's AI Mode shopping draws on its Shopping Graph, which is fed by product data such as Merchant Center listings, and OpenAI says shopping in ChatGPT is live in the US, with Shopify and Etsy catalogs already integrated and other merchants able to apply to share feeds. We make your product data complete, consistent and crawlable so these systems can use it.
