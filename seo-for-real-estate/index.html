Why most agent websites never rank.

04 · Neighborhood Pages

Hyperlocal neighborhood pages portals can't match.

A portal's neighborhood page is a listing grid with a few statistics. Yours can answer the questions a buyer actually asks before calling an agent: what the streets feel like, how long the commute takes at 8 a.m., what an HOA covers, which pockets flood, how the housing stock differs from one side of the main road to the other, and what recently sold and why. That is first-hand experience no feed can reproduce, and it is exactly the kind of content Google's ranking systems and AI assistants look for.

What goes on a strong neighborhood page

  • Your own photos and a short video walk-through, not stock images
  • Housing types, typical lot sizes, age of homes and HOA details
  • Commute times, parks, shopping and local landmarks
  • Links to official sources for schools, zoning and taxes rather than your opinion of them
  • Recent sales you were part of, with your commentary, plus a filtered IDX search for that area

Quality over quantity

Google's spam policies list "having multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as doorway abuse, and treat generating many pages without adding value for users as scaled content abuse. So we build pages only for areas you genuinely sell in, each with its own substance, and expand as you add listings and stories. Our SEO by city programs follow the same rule.

05 · IDX SEO

IDX and duplicate content, without the myths.

Your IDX feed shows the same MLS listings, with the same remarks and photos, that every other participating agent and the portals also show. Google does not publish a rule specific to IDX, but its general guidance is clear. When several pages are duplicates or very similar, Google chooses one URL as canonical and usually shows that one, and its spam policies say reproducing content feeds from other sites is a problem unless the site provides some unique benefit to users.

That is not a penalty for having IDX. It simply means raw listing pages rarely rank for you, and a site that is mostly IDX gives Google little reason to prefer it. Our approach to IDX SEO:

  • Keep IDX search on your own domain so visitors and inquiries stay with you
  • Make neighborhood and seller pages the pages you want ranked, with IDX results embedded as a useful extra
  • Add original commentary, photos and video to your own listings, which you can describe better than anyone
  • Use noindex on thin, endless search-result and filter URLs where it makes sense, so crawling focuses on pages with value
  • Set consistent canonical tags on your own pages so sorting and tracking parameters do not create copies

Every IDX vendor handles this differently, so we audit what your platform actually outputs before changing anything.

06 · Google Business Profile and Reviews

Agent profiles and reviews, set up by the rules.

Google's Business Profile guidelines name real estate agents as individual practitioners. An agent in a public-facing role who can be contacted at the verified location during stated hours can have their own profile, separate from the brokerage office's profile, and should not create extra profiles for each specialty. If you are the only public-facing agent at a branded office, Google says to share one profile named in the "Brand: Agent Name" format. Support staff should not have profiles.

Many agents work from home or on the road. Google says service-area businesses should hide a home address and list the areas they serve, generally within about two hours' drive. A virtual office or rented mailbox is not eligible, and business names must match the real-world name without keywords like "Best Realtor Austin." Breaking these rules is a common reason agent profiles get suspended. Our local SEO service covers profile setup, categories, photos and posts in full.

Reviews that hold up

Reviews are the most visible proof of an agent's track record, and closings give you a natural moment to ask. Google's review policy prohibits incentives such as discounts or gifts for reviews and prohibits selectively soliciting positive reviews, and the FTC's rule on consumer reviews, effective October 21, 2024, bans fake reviews. So we ask every closed client, buyers and sellers alike, and reply to every review. Our guide on how to get more Google reviews walks through the routine.

07 · Fair Housing

Fair Housing-safe content and ads.

Neighborhood content is where real estate SEO and fair housing law meet. HUD's Fair Housing Act regulation, 24 CFR 100.75, makes it unlawful to publish any notice, statement or advertisement for the sale or rental of a dwelling that indicates a preference, limitation or discrimination because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status or national origin. It covers words, phrases, photographs and illustrations, and the choice of where ads appear. Many states and cities protect additional groups.

In practice, we write neighborhood and listing pages that describe the property and the place, not the people who should live there. That means facts like square footage, walking distance to a park or a commute time, rather than phrases about the kind of buyer an area "suits." For schools and crime, we link to official data sources and let buyers judge for themselves.

Ads have their own rules. Google's personalized advertising policy says housing ads in the US and Canada cannot target by gender, age, parental status or marital status, or by ZIP code. Our Google Ads management follows those limits. We are not lawyers, so anything borderline goes to your broker or counsel before it is published.

11 · FAQ

Real estate SEO: common questions.

What is real estate SEO?

Real estate SEO is the work of helping agents, teams and brokerages get found when buyers and sellers search on Google, Google Maps and AI assistants. Instead of competing with Zillow and Realtor.com for listing searches, it focuses on the agent's Google Business Profile, hyperlocal neighborhood and seller pages, reviews, IDX and technical fixes, structured data and tracking leads back to the pages that produced them.

Can a real estate agent outrank Zillow?

Rarely for broad searches like homes for sale near me, where portals hold every top result. Agents can outrank portals on agent searches decided in the map pack, on specific neighborhood and community questions, on seller topics in their market and on their own name. That is where we focus.

Is IDX bad for SEO?

No. IDX is useful for visitors, and Google does not penalize a site for having it. But IDX listings are the same content shown on many other sites, and Google picks one version of duplicate or very similar pages to show, so raw listing pages rarely rank for you. Original neighborhood, seller and listing content is what earns rankings, with IDX results embedded as a helpful extra.

Should a realtor have their own Google Business Profile?

Often, yes. Google's guidelines list real estate agents as individual practitioners who can have their own profile if they are public facing and can be contacted at the verified location during stated hours. If you are the only public-facing agent at a branded office, Google says to share one profile named Brand: Agent Name. Agents who work from home should hide that address and list a service area.

How does the Fair Housing Act affect real estate SEO content?

HUD's regulation at 24 CFR 100.75 prohibits ads and statements about the sale or rental of a dwelling that indicate a preference, limitation or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status or national origin, including through words and images. Neighborhood pages should describe the property and the area rather than the kind of person who should live there, and housing ads on Google cannot target by age, gender, parental status, marital status or ZIP code.

How long does SEO for realtors take to work?

Google Business Profile and technical fixes can show results within weeks. Neighborhood pages, reviews and local authority usually take several months to move rankings, and large metros take longer than smaller towns. Nobody can guarantee a specific ranking, and we will not.

How much does a real estate SEO company cost?

It depends on your market, how many neighborhoods and agents you want to cover, and how much your website and IDX setup need. A solo agent in a smaller town needs far less than a multi-office brokerage in a large metro. We scope every engagement after a free audit, so you see the plan and the price before you commit.
