Most business owners ask "what digital marketing services does my business need" after a sales call where an agency offered six of them at once. SEO, Google Ads, social, email, content, a website rebuild, and now AI search. Each one can work. Buying all of them at the same time, before the basics are in place, is how small budgets disappear without a clear answer about what worked.

The digital marketing services your business needs depend on how your customers find and buy from you, and on what stage you are at. Almost every business should start with the same foundation: a website that converts, conversion tracking, and (for local businesses) a complete Google Business Profile. After that, add SEO or local SEO to capture existing search demand, paid search when you need leads sooner, email and automation to keep the customers you win, and AI search visibility once those layers are working.

How to use this guide: this is a decision framework, not a shopping list. We describe how we scope work for clients, and every platform fact is linked to Google, the SBA or another primary source. We do not quote price lists or average returns, because they vary too much by market to be useful. If you want a list of agencies instead, see our separate guide to the top digital marketing companies in the US.

Start With How Your Customers Buy, Not With a Service

A service is a tool. The right tool depends on the job. Before you compare SEO with paid ads, write one sentence that answers: "When someone needs what we sell, where do they look first, and what do they do before they pay?" That sentence usually points to one of four business models.

Local service businesses

Plumbers, dentists, HVAC companies, salons and law offices win most customers from people searching nearby, often on a phone, often with urgency. Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence, and that prominence draws on information such as links to your business and how many reviews you have. That makes a complete Google Business Profile and a steady flow of reviews the first job, followed by local SEO for your service and location pages. Our guide on how to get more Google reviews covers the review side. If you need calls this month, Google's Local Services Ads let eligible providers pay for leads related to the services they offer, rather than for clicks.

Ecommerce businesses

Online stores live or die on product pages, checkout and repeat purchases. Before any traffic spend, make sure purchases, revenue and add-to-cart events are tracked correctly, because every later decision (which ads to scale, which categories to write for) depends on that data. Then the usual order is product and category page SEO (our ecommerce SEO service covers this), Shopping and search ads for products with proven margins, and email marketing flows for abandoned carts and repeat buyers.

B2B and SaaS businesses

When a purchase involves a committee, a demo and weeks of research, buyers read a lot before they ever fill in a form. Content that answers their real problems, supported by SEO, does the heavy lifting. Paid search helps on high-intent terms like "[category] software pricing", but rarely carries the whole pipeline. The service that often gets skipped is the one that matters most after the first click: a CRM connected to marketing automation so leads are followed up, scored and nurtured instead of going cold in an inbox.

Professional services firms

Accountants, consultants, financial advisers and agencies sell trust. Referrals bring most of the work, but referred prospects still search your name and read what other sites and AI assistants say about you. Start with proof: clear service pages, case studies you can actually show, bios and reviews. Then add SEO for the questions prospects ask before they hire, and AI search visibility work so ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity describe your firm accurately when someone asks for a recommendation.

Franchises and multi-location brands are a mix of local and brand: each location needs its own profile and page, while the brand needs consistency. That is a separate problem, covered by our franchise SEO service.

The Foundation-First Order: Which Marketing Channel First?

Across those four models, the order of the first few services is surprisingly consistent. Each layer makes the next one cheaper or easier to judge. Skip one and you pay for it later, usually in ad spend you cannot attribute.

1. A website that converts, and tracking that proves it

Your website is where every other channel sends people. If a visitor cannot tell within a few seconds what you do, where you work and how to contact you, more traffic only produces more bounces. Pair it with measurement. In Google Analytics 4, a key event is an action "particularly important to the success of your business", such as a form submission, a call click or a purchase. Mark those before you spend a dollar on traffic. If your site already gets visitors but few inquiries, a CRO audit is usually a better first purchase than more traffic.

2. Google Business Profile (if you serve customers locally)

It is free, it appears in Maps and local results, and Google states that providing "complete and detailed business info" helps it match your profile to relevant searches. Categories, services, hours, photos and review replies all count. Online-only businesses can skip this step.

3. SEO and local SEO

SEO captures people already searching for what you sell. It is slow to start and compounding once it works. Google's own SEO Starter Guide says some changes "could take several months" to show, and suggests waiting "a few weeks" before judging whether a change helped. Plan for that timeline instead of expecting next-month results. Our step-by-step guide on how to SEO a website shows what the work involves.

4. Paid search

Paid search buys visibility immediately, which makes it the right tool for testing an offer, filling a gap while SEO matures, or covering high-value terms you cannot rank for yet. It stops the day you stop paying. It works far better after steps 1 and 3, because a converting landing page lowers your cost per lead and tracking tells you which keywords actually produce customers. See our Google Ads management service for how we run it.

5. Email and automation

Email turns one-time buyers into repeat buyers and slow leads into booked calls. It needs a list to work with, which is why it usually comes after you have some traffic. Simple automations (a welcome sequence, a quote follow-up, a review request after each job) often pay off faster than any new channel, because they work on people who already know you.

6. AI search visibility

More buyers now ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Google's AI Mode which provider to choose. Google's documentation on AI features in Search says there are "no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary" beyond normal SEO best practice. That is why it sits on top of the stack: AI visibility builds on a crawlable site, clear content and a good reputation. Once those exist, dedicated work on how AI assistants describe and recommend you is worth adding. Our explainer on GEO vs SEO covers where the two overlap and where they differ.

Which Services Fit Your Business Stage?

The same business needs different services at different stages. A simple rule keeps spending honest: add a new layer only when the one below it is working and measured.

  • Launch (new or rebuilding): website, tracking, Google Business Profile if local, and a short list of target keywords. If cash flow needs leads now, a small, tightly scoped paid search test on your most profitable service.
  • Traction (leads are coming in, inconsistently): SEO or local SEO for your core services, paid search for the offers that already convert, and a basic follow-up sequence for new leads.
  • Scale (reliable demand, growing team): deeper content, email and automation across the customer lifecycle, AI search visibility, and new markets or locations. This is also when a written digital marketing strategy pays for itself, because more channels mean more ways to waste money.

SEO vs PPC: Which Should Come First?

This is the most common version of "which marketing channel first". The honest answer is that they do different jobs.

How Should You Split a Marketing Budget?

We will not give you a percentage table, because any single number would be wrong for most readers. A personal injury firm and a candle shop should not split a budget the same way. What we can give you is the shape of the split and a method for setting it.

The U.S. Small Business Administration's guidance on making a marketing plan asks for "a complete breakdown of the costs" and recommends reviewing the plan at least annually, measuring ROI to see which parts are working. A practical way to apply that:

  1. Work backward from a customer. Estimate what a new customer is worth in gross profit, then decide what you can afford to pay to win one.
  2. Fund the foundation first. Tracking and a converting site are one-off costs that make every later dollar measurable.
  3. Size paid tests in plain numbers. Google Ads budgets are set daily, and Google says that in a billing period you are never charged more than 30.4 times your average daily budget. A $20 daily budget therefore caps a month at $608, which makes a test easy to plan.
  4. Review every quarter. Move money from channels with no measurable leads to the ones producing customers.

Signs You Are Buying the Wrong Service

Most wasted marketing spend is not on bad services. It is on the right service at the wrong time. Watch for these signals:

  • Traffic is up, leads are flat. You are paying to send people to a page that does not convert. Fix the site before buying more traffic.
  • Nobody can say which channel produced last month's customers. Tracking is missing. Every report you are reading is a guess.
  • You are running ads to a homepage. Paid clicks need a specific landing page that matches the search.
  • Your reports are mostly impressions, followers and "visibility". Those can be useful, but they are not leads, sales or revenue.
  • Someone promised a number one ranking. Google's own guidance on hiring an SEO is blunt: "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google."
  • You bought social or content before the basics. If your Business Profile is incomplete and your phone calls go unanswered after hours, the issue is not awareness.
  • Leads arrive and wait. If inquiries sit for hours, faster follow-up (automation or an AI lead agent) will likely do more than a new channel.

Questions to Ask Before You Hire Anyone

Whether you hire us, another agency or a freelancer, these questions separate a diagnosis from a sales pitch:

  • What problem in my funnel will this service fix, and how will we measure it?
  • What will you need from my team each month, in hours?
  • Which of my current channels would you cut or pause, and why?
  • When should we expect a first signal, and what result would make you recommend stopping?
  • Who owns the accounts, data and content if we part ways?

A good provider will sometimes tell you that you do not need their service yet. That is how we work too; our approach page explains how we test before we scale.

Frequently Asked Questions

What digital marketing services does my business need first?

Start with a website that converts and conversion tracking, plus a complete Google Business Profile if you serve customers locally. Then add SEO or local SEO for existing search demand, paid search if you need leads sooner, email and automation to keep customers, and AI search visibility once those layers work.

Should a small business start with SEO or PPC?

Choose PPC if you need leads this month or want to test an offer, and SEO if you can wait months for results that compound. Many small businesses run a small paid search campaign while SEO builds. Either way, set up conversion tracking first so you can judge the spend.

How many digital marketing services should I start with?

Usually one or two beyond the foundation. Adding a new channel before the current one is measured makes it hard to know what is working. Add the next layer only when the one below it is producing leads you can track.

How much should a small business spend on digital marketing?

There is no single right percentage. Work backward from what a new customer is worth in gross profit and what you can afford to pay to win one. The SBA recommends a full cost breakdown in your marketing plan and reviewing it at least annually based on ROI.

Does every business need social media marketing?

No. Social media works well for visual and consumer brands, but for many local service and B2B businesses it is rarely the first channel that produces customers. A complete Business Profile, reviews and fast lead follow-up usually come first.

Do I need AI search optimization?

If your buyers ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Google AI Mode for recommendations, yes, but build it on top of SEO. Google says there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode beyond normal SEO best practice, so a crawlable site and clear content come first.
