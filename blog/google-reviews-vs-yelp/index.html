Google reviews vs Yelp is not really a contest between two star ratings. The two platforms run on opposite rules: Google lets you ask every customer for a review, while Yelp tells businesses not to ask at all and filters reviews it thinks were requested. That one difference changes how you should spend your time on each, and it matters more now that Yelp content is licensed into ChatGPT and Perplexity.

For most US local businesses, Google reviews matter more, because Google says reviews help its local ranking and its reviews appear across Search and Maps. Yelp still matters for restaurants, nightlife and many home services, and it now reaches AI assistants through licensing deals. The practical answer: actively ask for Google reviews, keep a claimed and accurate Yelp page, reply on both, and never ask for Yelp reviews.

How we sourced this comparison: every policy and platform fact below comes from Google Business Profile Help, the Google Maps user contributed content policy, Yelp's Support Center and Trust & Safety pages, Yelp's investor release, Apple's Maps attribution page and reporting we could open and check on November 17, 2026. Where a popular claim could not be verified (for example, exact AI citation shares by platform), we left it out. The decision framework is our own recommendation, not a platform rule.

Where Google and Yelp Reviews Actually Show Up

The first thing to compare is reach: where a customer might read a review before they ever reach your website.

Google reviews: Search, Maps and Gemini summaries

Google reviews live on your Google Business Profile, which appears in the local pack, on Google Maps and in branded searches for your name. Google is also turning that review text into AI answers. When Gemini features arrived in Maps, Google told users they could "find out what people like about a place by reading the helpful review summarized with Gemini," as Search Engine Roundtable reported. In other words, what customers write on Google becomes the raw material for Google's own AI descriptions of your business.

Yelp reviews: Yelp, Apple Maps, ChatGPT and Perplexity

Yelp's reach is wider than its own app suggests, because Yelp licenses its content to other companies:

  • Apple Maps. Apple's official Maps data attribution page credits "Reviews from Yelp" to Yelp. Apple also runs its own native ratings: its support page on how to rate places and add photos in Maps describes Like and Dislike buttons by category, not star reviews, and notes the feature "isn't available in all countries or regions." So for iPhone users, written reviews in Maps can still come from Yelp.
  • ChatGPT. In its full-year 2025 results release, Yelp said it had "recently signed an agreement with OpenAI." By August 2026, Yelp described an existing integration that surfaces "Yelp's trusted reviews, ratings, photos, and business details directly within the chat experience," and added restaurant reservations and waitlists inside ChatGPT, as PYMNTS reported on August 10, 2026.
  • Perplexity. Since March 2024, Perplexity has shown Yelp "maps, reviews, photos, and more" inside its answers through Yelp's Fusion data licensing program, according to Maginative.

A licensing deal does not guarantee that an assistant will cite Yelp for your business in any given answer, and none of these companies publishes how it chooses sources. Treat Yelp as one of several inputs an assistant may read, not a switch that turns on AI visibility. Our guide to getting your brand recommended by ChatGPT covers the other inputs, and the ChatGPT Business Hub guide explains how to claim your listing directly with OpenAI.

The Biggest Difference: Asking for Reviews

This is where most comparison articles go soft, so here are the exact rules.

Google: asking is allowed, steering is not

Google's own tips to get more reviews tell owners to "remind customers to leave reviews" and say you "can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code." The Maps user contributed content policy sets the limits. Merchants may not offer incentives (payment, discounts, free goods or services) for a review, and may not "discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers." Google adds that merchants "should not require or pressure users to leave ratings or write reviews while on the premises," should not ask for specific content, and should not set staff quotas for reviews or reviews that name an employee. What Google does allow is asking for reviews that reflect a genuine experience, "without offering incentives to do so or attempting to influence the rating or the contents of the review."

Yelp: do not ask anyone

Yelp's Support Center article Don't Ask for Reviews is blunt: "Don't ask anyone to review your business, be it customers, mailing list subscribers, friends, family, etc." It also says staff should "never compete to collect reviews," and that you should not ask for reviews after collecting feedback through surveys or contact forms. Yelp's reason is that "many businesses only ask happy customers for reviews, which leads to biased ratings," so its software "tries to identify any reviews that appear to have been requested, and not recommend them." The same line appears in Yelp's Content Guidelines: "Businesses should never ask customers to write reviews."

One nuance: Yelp frames this as a content guideline enforced by software. A requested review is not illegal, but it is likely to land in the not recommended section, where it does not count toward your rating. The practical result is the same: asking wastes your customer's goodwill.

What both platforms (and the FTC) ban

Incentives are off the table everywhere. The FTC's final rule on fake reviews, announced August 14, 2024, prohibits compensation or incentives "conditioned on the writing of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment," and bars suppressing negative reviews through threats or intimidation. Yelp adds a public penalty: it may post a Consumer Alert on your page when it learns of this behavior.

How Yelp's Review Filter (Recommendation Software) Works

Owners often call it the Yelp review filter. Yelp calls it recommendation software, and says it is "entirely automated" and evaluates each review on "hundreds of signals of quality, reliability, and user activity." According to Yelp, it looks for:

  • Conflicts of interest, such as reviews from competitors, former employees, friends or family.
  • Solicited reviews that it suspects the owner or an employee asked for.
  • Reliability, which can mean a review from a less active user the software "doesn't know enough about."
  • Usefulness, screening out "unhelpful rants or raves."

Not recommended reviews stay visible through a link at the bottom of your page, "but they don't factor into the business's overall star rating or review count." Yelp says no employee can override the software and that advertisers and non-advertisers are treated the same. Its support article on why a review is not recommended adds that reviews from the same IP address or from a less active user are common reasons, and that reviews can move between sections over time.

The scale is real. Yelp's 2025 Trust & Safety Report, released February 25, 2026 (full release text via StockTitan), says that of roughly 22 million reviews posted in 2025, about 70% were recommended, 17% were not recommended, 11% were removed by Yelp's User Operations team and 2% were removed by the reviewers themselves. Yelp also filtered "nearly half a million reviews that exhibited characteristics of AI-generated content."

What this means for you: a sudden cluster of reviews from first-time Yelp users, especially right after you mention Yelp at checkout, is exactly the pattern the software is built to discount. Steady reviews from active Yelpers who found you on their own are what stick.

Who Uses Google and Yelp: Numbers You Can Check

Many "Google vs Yelp" articles quote consumer survey shares that are years old or behind a paywall. We stuck to figures from the platforms themselves.

  • Yelp's review base: Yelp users added 22 million reviews in 2025, "resulting in 330 million cumulative reviews, up 7% from the prior year," according to Yelp's investor release.
  • Where Yelp's business is growing: the same release reports Services advertising revenue up 8% to a record $948 million, while Restaurants, Retail & Other advertising fell 6% to $444 million. Yelp's own revenue mix leans toward home and local services, not only restaurants.
  • Google: Google does not publish a comparable count of Maps reviews, so we will not invent one. What Google does publish is how reviews affect ranking, which is the more useful fact for an owner (next section).

How Each Platform Feeds Local Rankings and AI Assistants

Local rankings

Google's guide to improving your local ranking says prominence is "based on info like how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have," and that "more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking." That is the clearest public statement any platform makes about reviews and visibility. Google does not say Yelp ratings feed the local pack, so do not expect Yelp reviews to move your Map Pack position directly.

Yelp still helps in two indirect ways. Your Yelp page is a citation with your name, address and phone, which supports the consistent business data our local SEO services work on. And a Yelp page can rank as its own result for "best [service] in [city]" style searches, giving you a second listing on page one.

AI assistants

Where AI assistants get reviews depends on the assistant. Google's Gemini features in Maps summarize Google reviews. ChatGPT and Perplexity have licensed Yelp content, and Apple Maps credits Yelp for reviews. None of these companies publishes how much weight each source gets, and third-party "AI citation share" studies disagree widely, so we do not quote one here. The safe conclusion is that a business with thin or stale reviews on either platform gives AI tools less to work with. Our AI search visibility work starts by checking what each assistant currently says about you and which sources it cites.

Industry Differences: Restaurants, Home Services and More

Restaurants, bars and cafes. Yelp is strongest here. The ChatGPT reservations and waitlist integration is built on Yelp Guest Manager, so a restaurant that uses it can be booked from inside a chat. Google reviews still drive Maps discovery, and Google's Gemini features in Maps can answer questions such as whether a place has outdoor seating, using what reviewers wrote.

Home services (HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical). Yelp's largest advertising segment is Services, so homeowners do use it to compare contractors. But most emergency searches happen on Google and Maps, where you can actively build volume. Ask for Google reviews after every completed job and keep Yelp accurate. See our guides to SEO for HVAC companies and SEO for electricians for the rest of the local stack.

Healthcare and dental. Both platforms matter less than getting replies right. In 2019, HHS settled with Elite Dental Associates in Dallas for $10,000 after the practice's reply to a Yelp review disclosed a patient's last name, treatment plan, insurance and cost details, as HIPAA Journal reported. Never confirm someone is a patient in a public reply on either platform. Our healthcare SEO page covers compliant reputation work.

Legal and professional services. Google reviews carry most of the weight because prospects search by practice area and city. Keep Yelp claimed and accurate, and reply professionally, but do not plan around it.

Multi-location brands and franchises. The policy split becomes an operations problem: location managers must know that Google review requests are fine and Yelp requests are not. Put it in the playbook. Our franchise SEO service handles this across locations.

How to Run Google and Yelp Reviews Together

  1. Claim both profiles. A Google Business Profile and a Yelp Business Page are both free. Match your name, address, phone, hours and categories exactly.
  2. Ask for Google reviews only. Send your Google review link to every customer the same way, every time. Our guide on how to get more Google reviews has the SMS, email and in-person templates.
  3. Never mention Yelp in a review request. That includes "review us on Google or Yelp" lines, survey follow-ups and staff contests.
  4. Make Yelp easy to find without asking. Keep photos, hours and services current so people who already use Yelp see a complete page.
  5. Reply on both platforms. Thank people specifically, answer complaints calmly and take details offline. Replies are visible to every future reader, and to AI tools that read the page.
  6. Check AI answers quarterly. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google about your category in your city and note which review sources they cite.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Google reviews or Yelp reviews more important?

For most US local businesses, Google reviews are more important because Google says reviews and ratings can help local ranking and they appear in Search and Maps. Yelp is close behind for restaurants and nightlife, and it matters for any business whose customers use iPhones or AI assistants that license Yelp content.

Can I ask customers to leave a Yelp review?

Yelp says no. Its Support Center tells businesses not to ask anyone, including customers, email subscribers, friends or family, and its recommendation software tries to filter reviews that appear to have been requested. You can ask for Google reviews, as long as you ask every customer and offer no incentive.

Why are my Yelp reviews not recommended?

Yelp's automated recommendation software may not recommend reviews from less active users, reviews that seem solicited, reviews from the same IP address or reviews with a possible conflict of interest. Not recommended reviews stay visible behind a link but do not count toward your rating. No Yelp employee can override the software, and advertising does not change the result.

Do Yelp reviews help my Google ranking?

Not directly, as far as Google has said. Google's local ranking guide mentions reviews in the context of your Business Profile, not Yelp. A Yelp page can still help indirectly as a consistent business citation and as a separate organic result for local searches.

Where do AI assistants get reviews from?

It depends on the assistant. Google summarizes Google reviews with Gemini in Maps. Yelp has licensing agreements with OpenAI for ChatGPT and with Perplexity, and Apple Maps credits Yelp for reviews. No assistant publishes how it weights each source, so keep both profiles accurate and active.

Does advertising on Yelp improve my reviews or rating?

No. Yelp says its recommendation software treats reviews of advertisers and non-advertisers exactly the same, and its employees cannot manually change which reviews are recommended. Advertising buys placement, not ratings.

Should I respond to reviews on both Google and Yelp?

Yes. Replies show future customers how you handle problems, and both platforms allow owners to respond publicly. Keep replies specific and calm, and in healthcare never confirm someone is a patient or mention treatment details.
