Most businesses asking how to get more Google reviews already have happy customers. What they lack is a system: a direct review link, a request sent at the right moment, and a habit of replying. This guide gives you all three, with SMS, email and in-person templates that stay inside Google's rules and the FTC's fake reviews rule.

To get more Google reviews, ask every real customer for an honest review soon after the purchase or service, send your direct Google review link or QR code by text, email, receipt or a card at the counter, keep the request short, and reply to the reviews you receive. Do not offer incentives, ask only happy customers, or have staff write reviews, because Google can remove those reviews and restrict your Business Profile.

How we sourced this guide: every policy point below comes from Google Business Profile Help, the Google Maps user-generated content policy and the Federal Trade Commission, each linked in place and checked on November 17, 2026. The templates and timing are our own recommendations from running review programs for local businesses, not Google requirements. Policies change, so recheck the linked pages before you train staff.

Why Google Reviews Matter for Local Rankings and AI Answers

Reviews do two jobs at once. They persuade the person reading your profile, and they feed the systems that decide whether that person sees you at all.

On rankings, Google is unusually direct. Its guide to improving your local ranking says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that prominence draws on "info like how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have." It then adds: "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking." The same page notes there is "no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google," so earning reviews is one of the few prominence levers fully in your hands.

On AI answers, review text is becoming source material. When Google rolled Gemini into Maps, it told users they could "find out what people like about a place by reading the helpful review summarized with Gemini" and ask Maps questions such as whether a place has outdoor seating, as Search Engine Roundtable reported. A summary can only repeat what customers wrote, so a steady flow of detailed, recent reviews gives AI tools accurate material to work with. ChatGPT is moving the same way: in its new ChatGPT Business Hub, a local business earns a mention when its location appears in a result. Our guide on how to get your brand recommended by ChatGPT covers the wider reputation signals AI assistants use.

Expect a lag: Google says that after a new review "it may take up to 2 weeks to get an updated review score," according to its page on review scores. Judge your program over months, not days.

What Google and the FTC Allow (and What Gets You Penalized)

Asking for reviews is allowed and encouraged. The Google Maps prohibited and restricted content policy says merchants may "solicit or encourage the posting of content that does represent a genuine experience, without offering incentives to do so or attempting to influence the rating or the contents of the review." The trouble starts when you try to steer the outcome.

The FTC rule on fake reviews and testimonials

The FTC announced its final rule banning fake reviews and testimonials on August 14, 2024. The rule, codified at 16 CFR Part 465, was published in the Federal Register on August 22, 2024, and "is effective October 21, 2024." It bans fake reviews (including AI-generated ones), buying reviews "conditioned on the writing of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment, either positive or negative," undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites, review suppression through "unfounded or groundless legal threats, physical threats, intimidation," and fake social media indicators. It also lets the FTC seek civil penalties against knowing violators.

The two rulebooks differ. The FTC's guide to soliciting reviews allows an incentive that is not conditioned on a positive review, as long as it is disclosed properly. Google does not allow any incentive, so for Google reviews the answer is simply no incentives at all.

What happens if you break the rules

Google removes violating reviews and can go further. Its page on Business Profile restrictions for policy violations lists examples: your profile may be blocked from receiving new reviews for a set period, your existing reviews may be unpublished for a set period, or your profile may show a warning "to let consumers know that fake reviews were removed."

How to Find and Share Your Google Review Link

Your Google review link sends customers straight to the review form, so they do not have to search for you. Google's help page on how to create a Google link or QR code to request reviews gives the steps:

  1. Sign in and go to your Business Profile on Google.
  2. Select Read reviews, then Get more reviews.
  3. Select Copy to copy your review link.
  4. To get the QR code, right-click it and choose Save image as. Google notes that review QR codes "can only be generated on a computer browser, not on mobile devices."

Google suggests putting the link on receipts, in thank-you emails and at the end of a chat, and printing the QR code for display in your store. Test the link on a phone signed out of your own Google account before you print anything. Customers do need to be signed in to a Google Account to post, and Google's tips to get more reviews note they can create one with a non-Gmail address.

When to Ask Customers for Reviews

Ask while the experience is fresh. For most local businesses, that means a verbal ask plus no more than two or three messages.

  • At handoff: when the job is finished, the meal is paid for or the appointment ends, a staff member asks in person and hands over a card or points to the QR code. The customer can review later. Google's policy says merchants "should not require or pressure users to leave ratings or write reviews while on the premises," so never stand over someone while they type.
  • Same day: a short text with the review link, sent within a few hours. For service businesses that finish jobs late, send it the next morning.
  • Day 2 or 3: an email version for customers who prefer email, or if the text was not opened.
  • About day 7: one polite reminder, then stop. Stop sooner if the customer has already reviewed or asks you to stop.

Tie the timing to a real event in your systems, such as an invoice marked paid, a job closed in your field service app or an order delivered. That is where marketing automation for review requests earns its keep: the request fires for every customer automatically, which also keeps you on the right side of the "no selective soliciting" rule. Make sure your texts follow your SMS consent and opt-out obligations; if you are unsure what consent you hold, use email until you check.

Review Request Templates You Can Copy

These templates follow the same rules: they go to every customer, ask for an honest review, offer nothing in return, and do not suggest a star rating or specific content. Replace the brackets and keep them short.

SMS review request template

Hi [First name], thanks for choosing [Business] today. If you have a minute, we'd appreciate an honest review on Google. It helps neighbors find us: [review link]
Reply STOP to opt out.

Email review request template

Subject: How did we do, [First name]?

Hi [First name],

Thank you for trusting [Business] with your [service or order]. We'd love to hear how it went. Would you share an honest review on Google? It takes about a minute, and every review, good or bad, helps us improve and helps other customers decide.

[Leave a review on Google] (button linked to your review link)

If something wasn't right, you can also reply to this email and I'll look into it personally.

Thanks,
[Your name], [Role]

The last line is fine because it offers help to everyone and still keeps the Google link in front of every customer. It turns into review gating only if you remove the Google option for unhappy customers or send them to a private form instead of Google.

In-person script

"Thanks again for coming in. If you get a chance later, we'd really appreciate an honest Google review. This card has a QR code that takes you straight there. No pressure at all."

Reminder template (send once)

Hi [First name], just a quick follow-up from [Business]. If you'd like to share your experience, here's our Google review link: [review link]. If you've already left one, thank you, and we won't ask again.

Phrases to avoid

  • "Leave us a 5-star review." This tries to influence the rating.
  • "Mention Sarah by name in your review." Google prohibits asking for reviews that identify a staff member.
  • "Get 10% off when you leave a review." Any incentive breaks Google policy.
  • "If you were happy, review us on Google; if not, tell us privately." That is review gating.

How to Respond to Google Reviews

Replying is the other half of the system. Google's guide to managing customer reviews explains that you can reply only after your business is verified, that replies are checked against Google's content policies (usually within 10 minutes, but sometimes up to 30 days), that replies appear as your business rather than your personal name, and that the reviewer is notified and "can still change their review after reading your reply."

Google's reply tips boil down to this: be professional and brief, skip generic copy-paste thanks and promotions, never share a reviewer's private information, apologize when appropriate, and move complex issues to phone or email.

Response templates

Positive review:

Thanks, [Name]. We're glad the [specific detail they mentioned] worked out, and we appreciate you taking the time to write this. See you next time. [Your name], [Business]

Negative review about a real problem:

[Name], thank you for telling us, and I'm sorry the [issue] fell short. That's not the standard we aim for. I'd like to make this right. Please call me at our main number or email [address] and mention this review. [Your name], [Role]

Review from someone you cannot find in your records:

Hi [Name], we take feedback seriously, but we can't find a visit or order that matches this review. Could you contact us at [address] so we can look into it? We want to help if something went wrong.

If you work in a regulated field such as healthcare or legal services, do not confirm that the reviewer is a patient or client, even if they say so. Our guide to SEO for healthcare covers what else changes for medical practices.

What to Do About Review Bombing and Fake Reviews

Review bombing is a sudden burst of negative reviews that do not reflect real experiences, from a competitor, an online pile-on or a scammer.

Report reviews that break policy

Google's guide to reporting inappropriate reviews says to go to your Business Profile, select Read reviews, select Report next to the review, choose a reason such as "Spam" or "Profanity," and send the report. Evaluation "typically takes several days," and you can track the status in the Reviews Management Tool. Google says not to report a review "just because you disagree with it." Report reviews that are fake, from someone with a conflict of interest, off-topic, or abusive. The Maps policy also excludes "general, political, or social commentary or personal rants," which covers many reviews posted by people reacting to a news story rather than a visit.

If someone demands money to remove reviews

Google has a separate process for negative review extortion scams, which it describes as "a sudden increase in 1-star and 2-star reviews" followed by a demand for "money, goods, or services in exchange for removing the negative reviews." Its instructions are clear: do not engage with or pay the people involved, gather evidence immediately (screenshots showing dates, times and sender details, plus links to each review), and submit the merchant extortion report form.

A calm review bombing checklist

  1. Screenshot every suspicious review with its date and reviewer name.
  2. Check your records. Separate real customers from accounts you cannot match.
  3. Report the clear violations individually, choosing the most accurate reason.
  4. If there is a demand for money, use the extortion form and do not reply to the demand.
  5. Post one short, neutral reply on the worst reviews so readers see you are responsive. Do not accuse anyone.
  6. Keep your normal review requests running. A steady flow of genuine reviews is the best defense against a short spike.

Build a Review System That Runs Every Week

One-off review pushes create spikes, and Google's policy flags "unusual volumes or patterns of review contributions." A steady system is safer and works better. Here is the setup we use with local clients:

Inbound conversations are a second source of requests. If you use an AI assistant to answer calls, chats or texts, it can send the review link after a booking is completed, which Google lists as a good moment ("at the end of a chat interaction"). Frequently Asked Questions

Is it okay to ask customers for Google reviews?

Yes. Google allows businesses to ask for reviews that reflect a genuine experience, as long as they offer no incentives and do not try to influence the rating or content. Ask every customer, not only happy ones.

How do I get my Google review link?

In your Business Profile, select Read reviews, then Get more reviews, then Copy. The review QR code is on the same screen, but Google says it can only be generated in a computer browser.

Can I offer a discount or gift for a Google review?

No. Google prohibits payment, discounts or free goods or services in exchange for any review, even if you do not ask for a positive one. The FTC rule separately bans incentives conditioned on a particular sentiment.

What is review gating, and is it allowed?

Review gating means screening customers and sending only satisfied ones to Google. It is not allowed: Google prohibits selectively soliciting positive reviews, and FTC guidance says not to ask only customers you expect to be positive.

Should I respond to every Google review?

Read every review and reply to most, especially negative ones and those with questions. Google advises against generic copy-paste thank-yous, so keep replies short, specific and free of promotions or private information.

Can I get a negative Google review removed?

Only if it violates Google's policies, such as spam, fake content, a conflict of interest or harassment. Report it from your Business Profile. For extortion demands, use Google's merchant extortion report form and never pay.

Do more Google reviews improve local rankings?

They can. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking as part of prominence, alongside relevance and distance.

Where to Start This Week

Copy your Google review link today, test it on a phone, and print a QR card for the counter. Pick the trigger that fires your request for every customer, load the SMS and email templates above, and block 15 minutes a day to reply to new reviews. Within a month you will know your request-to-review rate, and that number tells you what to fix next.
