01 · Why Healthcare Is Different

Why medical websites are held to a higher standard.

Health searches are not like searches for pizza or plumbers. Google says its systems give even more weight to content with strong E-E-A-T (experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trustworthiness) on topics that can affect someone's health, financial stability or safety, which it calls "Your Money or Your Life" (YMYL) topics. The same page says trust is the most important of the four.

That changes how healthcare SEO should be done. Thin symptom articles written by an anonymous copywriter, stock photos of doctors who do not work at the practice and copy-paste location pages are exactly what patients and search systems learn to ignore. What works is slower and more honest: real providers with verifiable credentials, content reviewed by clinicians, accurate listings, and a site that respects patient privacy. 03 · YMYL and E-E-A-T

How do you show E-E-A-T on a medical website?

Google is careful to say that E-E-A-T is not a specific ranking factor. Its helpful content guidance explains that search quality raters are trained to judge whether content has strong E-E-A-T, and that raters have no control over how pages rank: their ratings help Google check whether its systems are returning good results. So the goal is not to sprinkle "trust signals" on a page. It is to make the real expertise behind your practice visible and easy to verify.

Provider pages with real credentials

Every physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant and therapist who sees patients gets a page with their degree, board certifications, residency and fellowship training, hospital affiliations, specialties, languages, locations and insurance accepted. Patients compare doctors on exactly these details, and a clear provider page is the page AI assistants and Google can quote when someone asks about a specific doctor.

Content reviewed by clinicians

Condition and treatment pages name the clinician who reviewed them and show when they were last reviewed. We write for patients first: what the condition is, when to seek care, what to expect at a visit, how treatment works and what it typically involves. We avoid promising outcomes, and we keep pages free of claims your clinicians would not stand behind.

Proof that the practice is real

Accurate addresses, hours, insurance lists, photos of your actual team and facility, and consistent details across your site, Google and directories. Simple things, but they are what patients and search systems use to decide whether to trust you.

04 · Local SEO for Practices

Google Business Profiles for practices and individual providers.

Most patients choose care close to home, so the map pack is where many appointments start. Healthcare also has its own listing rules. Google's guidelines treat doctors and dentists as individual practitioners who may have their own profile when they are public-facing and can be contacted directly at the verified location during stated hours. When several practitioners work at one location, the practice should have its own profile, separate from the practitioners' profiles, and each practitioner's profile title should include only their name. A solo practitioner at a branded practice is better off sharing one profile in the "Brand: Practitioner Name" format.

For a multi-provider clinic, that means cleaning up duplicates, merging or removing profiles for doctors who have left, making sure support staff do not have profiles, and connecting each provider profile to the right page on your site. Google also says a practitioner should not have multiple profiles to cover different specializations. Our local SEO service covers this setup, plus categories, services, photos and citations on the health directories patients use.

Multi-location groups and hospital SEO

Hospitals and health systems need a clean structure: a page for each location and each service line, a findable provider directory, and listings for departments that genuinely take patients directly, such as urgent care or imaging. Groups with many sites can pair this with our multi-location SEO approach so every location is consistent without duplicate content.

05 · Medical Schema

Structured data that describes your practice accurately.

Schema.org has types built for healthcare. MedicalClinic sits under both LocalBusiness and MedicalOrganization and supports properties such as medicalSpecialty, availableService, healthPlanNetworkId and isAcceptingNewPatients. Physician describes a doctor or a physician's office and supports hospitalAffiliation, medicalSpecialty and usNPI, the 10-digit National Provider Identifier issued by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services.

We mark up each location and provider page with the properties you can honestly fill, keep the data identical to what patients see on the page, and connect providers to their locations. Schema helps search engines and AI systems understand who you are and what you offer. It does not guarantee rich results or rankings, and anyone who says otherwise is overselling. 06 · HIPAA-Aware Tracking

Measuring results without leaking patient information.

Analytics and ad pixels are where many healthcare marketing programs take on real risk. The HHS Office for Civil Rights bulletin on use of online tracking technologies by HIPAA covered entities and business associates says tracking technologies on user-authenticated pages, such as patient portals and telehealth platforms, generally have access to protected health information (PHI), and that information collected through a regulated entity's mobile app generally is PHI. Where a tracking vendor receives PHI on your behalf, the bulletin says you need a permitted disclosure and a business associate agreement (BAA) with that vendor.

One part of that guidance no longer stands. On June 20, 2024, the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, in American Hospital Association v. Becerra, vacated the portion saying HIPAA obligations are triggered when an online technology connects an individual's IP address with a visit to an unauthenticated public webpage about specific health conditions or providers. HHS notes the ruling at the top of the bulletin, and the AHA reports that HHS withdrew its appeal on August 29, 2024, making the decision final. The rest of the bulletin, including the guidance on authenticated pages and apps, remains, and state privacy laws still apply.

In practice, we keep marketing tags off portals, appointment forms and anything that captures symptoms or patient details unless your compliance team has approved the vendor and a BAA is in place. We prefer aggregate conversion counts and server-side or HIPAA-eligible tools your team selects. We are SEO specialists, not lawyers: we flag the risks we see and work with your compliance officer or counsel on the decisions.

07 · Reviews and Patient Privacy

Replying to patient reviews without breaking HIPAA.

Reviews influence which practice a patient calls, and replying to them shows you care. But a reply must never confirm that the reviewer is a patient or mention anything about their care. In a December 2022 settlement, a California dental practice paid $23,000 and agreed to a corrective action plan after replies to Yelp reviews disclosed patients' full names and details about their visits, treatment and insurance, as reported by HIPAA Journal. OCR Director Melanie Fontes Rainer said providers cannot disclose patients' protected health information when responding to negative online reviews.

We give your team reply templates that stay general ("We take every concern seriously. Please call our office manager so we can help."), move the conversation offline, and never reference a visit, procedure or diagnosis. For asking, we set up a simple post-visit review request for every patient, never only the happy ones, and we never buy or write reviews: the FTC's rule on consumer reviews, effective October 21, 2024, prohibits fake reviews and paying for positive ones.

12 · FAQ

Healthcare SEO: common questions.

What is healthcare SEO?

Healthcare SEO is the work of helping medical practices, clinics and hospitals get found when patients search for care on Google, Google Maps and AI assistants. It covers provider and service-line pages, clinician-reviewed content, Google Business Profiles for each location and provider, medical schema, technical fixes, reviews and measurement that respects patient privacy.

How is medical SEO different from regular SEO?

Health is a Your Money or Your Life topic, and Google says its systems give even more weight to content with strong experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trustworthiness on those topics. Medical SEO therefore depends on named, credentialed providers, clinician-reviewed and dated content, accurate listings, and privacy rules such as HIPAA that do not apply to most other businesses.

Should each doctor have their own Google Business Profile?

Often, yes. Google's guidelines say a public-facing practitioner who can be contacted directly at the verified location during stated hours may have their own profile, separate from the practice's profile, titled with only their name. A solo practitioner at a branded practice should usually share one profile in the Brand: Practitioner Name format, and support staff should not have profiles.

Is Google Analytics or a Meta pixel HIPAA compliant on a medical website?

It depends on where the tags run and what they collect. HHS guidance says tracking on user-authenticated pages such as patient portals generally has access to protected health information, which requires a permitted disclosure and a business associate agreement with the vendor. A June 2024 federal court ruling vacated the part of that guidance covering IP addresses on unauthenticated public pages, but state laws still apply. Review your setup with your compliance officer or counsel.

Can a practice respond to patient reviews?

Yes, but replies must never confirm that the reviewer is a patient or mention their visit, treatment or insurance. In a 2022 settlement, a dental practice paid $23,000 after its Yelp replies disclosed patient information. Keep replies general, thank the reviewer, and invite them to contact the office directly.

How long does healthcare SEO take to work?

Google Business Profile, listing and technical fixes can show results within weeks. Provider pages, clinician-reviewed content and reviews usually take several months to move rankings, and competitive specialties in large metros take longer. Nobody can guarantee a specific ranking, and we will not.

How much does healthcare SEO cost?

It depends on the number of locations, providers and service lines, how competitive your specialty and market are, and how much your website needs. A single-location practice needs far less than a multi-site health system. We scope every engagement after a free audit, so you see the plan and the price before you commit.
