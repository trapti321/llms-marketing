04 — Local SEO

Local SEO for HVAC companies, done by the rules.

Most HVAC contractors travel to the customer, which makes them service-area businesses in Google's eyes. Google's guidelines say service-area businesses should hide their address and list the areas they serve, and that business names should reflect the real-world name, without added keywords. A showroom or shop that is staffed during business hours can show its address and a service area too.

We set your profile up this way, then grow relevance and prominence with complete services, real job photos, a steady flow of genuine reviews and pages for the towns you actually cover. We never use virtual offices or keyword-stuffed names. They break Google's guidelines and can get a profile suspended in the middle of peak season.

Reviews homeowners trust

We set up a simple routine to request a review after every completed call and reply to every one. We never buy or write reviews: the FTC's rule on consumer reviews, effective October 21, 2024, prohibits fake reviews and paying for positive ones.

06 — Accurate, Current Content

Pages that keep up with rules, refrigerants and rebates.

HVAC content goes out of date quickly, and stale pages cost trust. Two current examples:

  • Federal tax credits changed. The IRS says the energy efficient home improvement credit, which covered qualifying improvements such as heat pumps, is allowed for property placed in service on or after January 1, 2023, and before December 31, 2025 (IRS). Pages still promising that credit for a new installation mislead homeowners; we update them to point to current utility and state programs instead.
  • Refrigerant rules moved. In May 2026, EPA lifted the federal January 1, 2026 installation deadline for residential and light commercial R-410A equipment made before 2025, while New York's state deadline remains in effect, as reported by the Air Conditioning Contractors of America (ACCA). Homeowners ask about this, and clear, dated answers build trust.

We also make your credentials easy to verify: state contractor licenses, insurance, manufacturer dealer status and technician certifications. Technicians who maintain or repair equipment that could release refrigerants must be certified under the EPA's Section 608 program, and saying so plainly is a simple trust signal many competitors skip.

11 — FAQ

HVAC SEO: common questions.

What is HVAC SEO?

HVAC SEO is the work of getting a heating and cooling company found when homeowners and businesses search for HVAC services nearby, on Google Maps, in organic results and in AI assistants. It covers the Google Business Profile, a page for each service such as AC repair, furnace repair, installs and maintenance plans, service-area pages for towns you genuinely cover, reviews, technical fixes and tracking calls back to the searches that produced them.

What do HVAC SEO services include?

Our HVAC SEO services include service, season and town keyword research, Google Business Profile optimization, service and service-area pages, technical SEO and HVACBusiness schema, citations and local links, AI search visibility testing, conversion and call-handling improvements, and monthly reporting on calls and booked jobs by service and town.

How long does SEO for HVAC companies take to work?

Google Business Profile and technical fixes can show results within weeks. New service pages, reviews and local authority usually take several months to move rankings, and competitive metros take longer than smaller towns. Because HVAC demand is seasonal, we publish heating and cooling pages ahead of their peak. Nobody can guarantee a specific ranking, and we will not.

Should an HVAC company show its address on Google?

Most HVAC companies travel to customers, which makes them service-area businesses under Google's guidelines. Google says service-area businesses should hide their address and list the areas they serve instead. If customers visit a staffed showroom or shop during business hours, you can show that address and add a service area as well.

Is local SEO or Local Services Ads better for HVAC?

They do different jobs. Local Services Ads and Google Ads put you in front of homeowners immediately and charge per lead or per click, which gets expensive in peak season. Local SEO builds map pack and organic visibility you do not pay for each time someone calls. Most HVAC companies do best with SEO as the foundation and ads to fill gaps.

Why hire an HVAC SEO company instead of a general agency?

An HVAC SEO agency should already understand seasonal demand, the service-area rules for Google Business Profiles, the difference between repair calls and replacement sales, and topics homeowners ask about such as refrigerant changes and rebates. Whoever you hire, ask how they report booked jobs rather than rankings alone.

How much does SEO for HVAC contractors cost?

It depends on your market, the services and towns you want to rank for and how much your website needs. A single-location contractor in a smaller town needs far less than a multi-location company in a large metro. We scope every engagement after a free audit, so you see the plan and the price before you commit.
